Introduction to Social Psychology

This module explores how social contexts influence individual behavior, attitudes, and cognition. Students will learn about conformity, social roles, group dynamics, persuasion, and interpersonal relationships.

Unit 1: Social Influence and Conformity

Subtopics:

  • Definition and scope of social psychology
  • Conformity and compliance
  • Obedience and groupthink
  • Social norms and influence on behavior
Example:
Scenario: Participants in a study adjust their answers to match the majority opinion even when it is clearly wrong (Asch Conformity Experiment). This demonstrates social influence.
Exercise:
  • Observe a classroom or group setting. Identify instances of conformity and explain why individuals followed the group.
  • Write a short reflection on a time you complied with group pressure and analyze the social factors involved.

Unit 2: Self-Concept and Interpersonal Relationships

Subtopics:

  • Self-concept and self-esteem
  • Interpersonal relationships and impression formation
  • Attitudes: ABC model (Affect, Behavior, Cognition)
  • Attitude formation, change, and persuasion
Example:
Observation: A new student forms impressions of classmates based on appearance and behavior. Attitudes change over time through interaction and information.
Exercise:
  • Create an ABC model for a personal attitude (e.g., toward a subject, activity, or social issue).
  • Interview a friend and map how their self-concept influences their interactions and decisions.

Unit 3: Groups and Behavior in Social Contexts

Subtopics:

  • Types of groups and group dynamics
  • Behavior in the presence of others: social facilitation and social loafing
  • Group decision-making, polarization, and conformity
Example:
Scenario: In a team project, some members contribute less when they feel their effort is less noticeable (social loafing). Discussion reveals how group size and accountability affect participation.
Exercise:
  • Participate in a small group activity and observe how group size affects individual contributions.
  • Discuss examples of groupthink in historical or current events and identify causes and consequences.

Unit 4: Cognitive Dissonance and Persuasion

Subtopics:

  • Cognitive dissonance and its effects
  • Persuasion techniques and attitude change
  • Social cognition and information processing
Example:
Scenario: A student believes smoking is harmful but smokes occasionally. To reduce dissonance, they justify it as “stress relief” or “occasional harmless indulgence.”
Exercise:
  • Identify a personal experience of cognitive dissonance and explain how you resolved it.
  • Analyze an advertisement or political speech for persuasion techniques used to influence attitudes.
