Research Module

This module guides students through the structured process of research. You will learn to formulate research questions, collect and analyze data, interpret results, and present findings effectively.

Unit 1: Formulation of Research Question

Subtopics:

  • Observation and Exploration
  • Discussion and brainstorming
  • Raising meaningful research questions
  • Understanding the scope and limitations
Example:
Observation: Students are spending increasing time on mobile devices.
Research Question: How does screen time affect students’ sleep patterns?
Exercise:
  • Observe your surroundings and identify a problem or phenomenon.
  • Formulate 2-3 research questions related to your observation.
  • Discuss with peers which question is most researchable and why.

Unit 2: Data Collection & Sources

Subtopics:

  • Primary Data: Surveys, interviews, experiments, field studies
  • Secondary Data: Library, journal papers, online articles, case studies
  • Qualitative vs Quantitative Data
  • Sampling and determining sample size
Example:
Topic: Effect of exercise on mental health
Primary Data: Conduct a survey of 50 students on weekly exercise habits and stress levels.
Secondary Data: Review existing studies on exercise and mental health outcomes.
Exercise:
  • Identify 2 primary data collection methods for your research question.
  • Identify 2 reliable secondary sources for the same question.
  • Determine a sample size and justify why it is sufficient.

Unit 3: Data Handling & Analysis

Subtopics:

  • Segregation, clustering, sequencing of data
  • Finding patterns and classifying information
  • Using tables, graphs, diagrams, and charts
  • Correlating data and making inferences
Example:
Survey Data: Students’ sleep hours and study hours
Analysis: Create a scatter plot to find correlation between study hours and sleep quality. Identify patterns and anomalies.
Exercise:
  • Organize sample data from your research question into a table.
  • Create a bar chart or pie chart to represent your data visually.
  • Write a brief analysis identifying any patterns or correlations.

Unit 4: Interpretation & Presentation

Subtopics:

  • Making conclusions from data
  • Linking findings back to research question
  • Presentation techniques: reports, charts, slides
  • Citing sources and acknowledging references
Example:
Data Analysis: Students with higher exercise frequency report lower stress levels.
Conclusion: Regular exercise is associated with reduced stress among students.
Presentation: Use charts and bullet points in a report, cite survey and literature references.
Exercise:
  • Write a 1-paragraph conclusion from your analyzed data.
  • Prepare a simple chart or diagram summarizing key findings.
  • List all sources used and ensure proper acknowledgment.
