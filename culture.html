Culture and Society

This module explores human culture and society through anthropological and sociological perspectives. Students will examine social structures, norms, roles, and governance systems, gaining a deep understanding of how culture shapes human experience.

Unit 1: Understanding Culture and Society

Subtopics:

  • Defining culture and society
  • Formation of society and culture
  • Enculturation and acculturation
  • Social stratification: caste, class, and gender
  • Gender roles, ideologies, and hierarchies
Example:
Observing a family structure: Compare a patriarchal family versus a matriarchal family and analyze how gender roles affect decision-making and responsibilities.
Exercise:
  • Identify examples of enculturation in your own life (traditions, rituals, or beliefs).
  • Analyze a social hierarchy in your community or school and discuss its impact on behavior and opportunities.

Unit 2: Governance, Power, and Social Events

Subtopics:

  • Governance and power structures
  • Constitutional ideals: equality, freedom, and duties
  • Historically significant events and their societal impact
  • Patriarchal vs matriarchal governance in families and societies
Example:
Examine the impact of a historical social reform (e.g., abolition of a discriminatory practice) and discuss how it changed power dynamics in society.
Exercise:
  • Research a local historical event and explain its effect on society and governance.
  • Discuss in groups how power and gender roles influence decision-making in families or communities.

Unit 3: Family, Marriage, and Care

Subtopics:

  • Domestic life and types of family
  • Marriage and its social functions
  • Caring for children, elderly, sick, and disabled
Example:
Compare joint vs nuclear family structures and their effect on childcare and eldercare responsibilities.
Exercise:
  • Write a short essay analyzing how your family structure affects daily responsibilities.
  • Identify cultural norms regarding marriage and discuss their advantages and limitations.

Unit 4: Cultural Relativism and Perspectives

Subtopics:

  • Cultural relativism and understanding other cultures
  • Emic and etic approaches in anthropology
  • Gender, ideology, and exploitation
Example:
Analyze a cultural practice different from your own using both emic (insider) and etic (outsider) perspectives to understand its meaning and function.
Exercise:
  • Observe a cultural festival or tradition and explain it from an emic perspective.
  • Discuss a social issue in your community and evaluate it using cultural relativism principles.
