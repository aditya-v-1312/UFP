Elementary Economics

This module introduces the fundamental concepts of economics, including scarcity, economic growth, utility, demand, and basic economic reasoning. Students will learn how individuals, businesses, and societies make choices and allocate resources.

Unit 1: Introduction to Economics

Subtopics:

  • Definitions of economics: Wealth, Welfare, Scarcity, Economic Growth
  • Economic activities and decision-making
  • Understanding human wants: characteristics and classification
  • Utility: meaning and forms
Example:
Scenario: A person has a limited budget but wants to buy both a book and a coffee. Economics helps analyze how to allocate money efficiently to maximize satisfaction (utility).
Exercise:
  • List 5 examples of scarce resources in your daily life and suggest how you would allocate them.
  • Identify different types of human wants around you and classify them as basic, luxury, or collective.

Unit 2: Economic Theory and Analysis

Subtopics:

  • Nature and applications of economic theory
  • Positive vs normative economics
  • Inductive and deductive methods
  • Basic concepts of goods and utility
Example:
Observation: A market analyst uses historical sales data (inductive method) to predict future trends, while policymakers may apply theoretical models (deductive method) to forecast economic outcomes.
Exercise:
  • Identify a news article discussing an economic policy. Determine whether it represents positive or normative economics.
  • Provide examples of inductive reasoning in everyday decisions (e.g., choosing a restaurant based on past experiences).

Unit 3: Law of Demand and Determinants

Subtopics:

  • Understanding demand and the law of demand
  • Determinants of demand: price, income, preferences
  • Introduction to market behavior and choices
Example:
Scenario: When the price of ice cream decreases, more people buy it, demonstrating the law of demand. Other factors like income, season, and tastes also affect demand.
Exercise:
  • Observe 3 products in a shop. Predict how demand might change if the price rises or falls and explain why.
  • Identify non-price factors influencing demand for your favorite product.

Unit 4: Applications of Economics

Subtopics:

  • Resource allocation in households and markets
  • Understanding trade-offs and opportunity cost
  • Decision-making in limited-resource situations
Example:
Scenario: A student has 2 hours to study or work on a hobby. Choosing study involves an opportunity cost of not pursuing the hobby. Economics helps evaluate such trade-offs.
Exercise:
  • Identify a decision you made this week involving scarce resources. Explain the trade-off and opportunity cost.
  • Analyze a local market (e.g., vegetables or mobile phones) and describe how sellers allocate resources to meet demand.
