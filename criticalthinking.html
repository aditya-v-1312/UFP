Critical Thinking Module

This module introduces students to the systematic processes of critical thinking. You will learn to evaluate arguments, analyze information, question assumptions, and reason logically.

Unit 1: Assessment and Evaluation

Subtopics:

  • Clarifying the purpose of information
  • Checking accuracy of statements
  • Questioning assumptions
  • Using logic to form inferences
Example:
Statement: “All birds can fly. Penguins are birds. Therefore, penguins can fly.”
Analysis: Identify assumptions (all birds can fly), check accuracy (penguins cannot fly), evaluate the logic (invalid inference).
Exercise:
  • Statement: “All fruits have seeds. Tomatoes are fruits. Therefore, tomatoes have seeds.” Evaluate assumptions and logic.
  • Statement: “Everyone likes chocolate. Sam likes chocolate. Therefore, Sam is happy.” Identify assumptions.
  • Statement: “All dogs are pets. Max is a dog. Max is a pet.” Evaluate reasoning.

Unit 2: Analysis and Reasoning

Subtopics:

  • Identifying elements to draw conclusions
  • Forming conjectures and hypotheses
  • Considering relevant information
  • Checking implications
Example:
Article snippet: “A company claims its product increases productivity by 30%.”
Analysis: Evaluate the evidence provided, identify missing data, consider alternative explanations, and reason whether the claim is valid.
Exercise:
  • Analyze a news paragraph and identify the main argument, supporting evidence, and any assumptions.
  • Read a short editorial and summarize the reasoning steps leading to the conclusion.

Unit 3: Case Studies & Real-Life Applications

Subtopics:

  • Group discussion on problem-solving
  • Art work interpretation
  • Reflective exercises
Example:
Scenario: “A city wants to ban cars downtown to reduce pollution. Some citizens oppose it.”
Analysis: Evaluate arguments for and against the ban, check logic and relevance of each point, and summarize a reasoned conclusion.
Exercise:
  • Write a paragraph evaluating a local news report using critical thinking steps.
  • Discuss in groups the pros and cons of a proposed policy, identify assumptions and implications.

Unit 4: Practical Logic Exercises

Subtopics:

  • Logical puzzles
  • Deductive and inductive reasoning
  • Identifying biases
Example:
“All mammals breathe air. Dolphins are mammals. Do dolphins breathe air?”
Analysis: Deductive reasoning → Yes, dolphins breathe air.
Exercise:
  • Create 2-3 short logical puzzles and solve them using deductive reasoning.
  • Read a statement and identify any hidden bias or assumption.
